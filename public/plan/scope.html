1The whole idea on one page

2The Unique Kernel

AI proposes, code disposes. A vision model may only pick from a short, fixed list of problem types and must say how sure it is. A cited rules table — never the model — decides the NYC hazard class and the legal correction window. The photo, its capture time and a content hash become an evidence entry, and the building’s public violation history shows the tenant isn’t alone. Photo → code class → legal clock → building history → notice does not exist as one flow today.

3Who it’s for

A New York City renter

Typically in an older (often rent-stabilized) building, with a leak, mold, no heat, pests, a broken lock or peeling paint.

Today

They text or call the landlord, keep no dated record, and don’t know what the Housing Maintenance Code requires or how long the landlord has to fix it.

When it escalates

To a 311 complaint, an HPD inspection or housing court, they have a scattered camera roll and no paper trail.

Secondary

Tenant-association organizers and legal-aid intake workers who need structured evidence.

“Every repair dispute turns on documentation.”

4The core loop

Open the app on a phone → snap the problem (plus one optional line like “ceiling dripping since Tuesday”) → see the class card with its clock and the “why” → it lands on a dated timeline. They come back to add another issue or a follow-up (“still broken on day 12”), and when ready, produce the notice.

  2. 2 AI proposes

5What “working” looks like

On a phone: enter an address once, photograph a stained ceiling that is no longer dripping, and watch it flip into a stamped “Class B · Hazardous · 30-day correction window” card with a running countdown and the cited code section. The building panel slides in: “This building already has N open violations, M for leaks.” The issue lands on a dated timeline with its hash and timestamps, and one tap produces a print-ready notice PDF.

How this plan was made

The answers came from the builder’s private research brief rather than a live interview; the builder approved by asking for the full build. Decisions the builder made in the build sessions: the doodle kit plus violet for the AI; later, dropping the neon lime for matching felt-tip colours and adding a dark theme; subagents matched to task size; a direct Gemini key within free-tier limits; a public repo with Vercel auto-deploys; and, after the first build, adding what a renter needs next (heat log, landlord on record, next steps, backup, offline). Full list: scope.md.

8Scope change after the first build

The proof of concept above shipped first and is unchanged. The builder then asked for more functionality and, later, features beyond this plan. They now ship alongside it, outside the boundary above, and are awaiting the builder's review and approval. The checklist's Revisions log gives the reasons.

  • Heat and hot water log (indoor readings checked against §27-2029/§27-2031; outdoor temperature from the National Weather Service)
  • Landlord on record and an addressed notice; the landlord track record across seven NYC Open Data sets
  • Ask the law: answers only in quotes, each checked word for word by code
  • Message coach: the AI writes the words, code fills and checks every fact
  • Next steps and rights; backup and restore; calendar reminders; offline; dark theme
